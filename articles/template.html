Your Article Title Goes Here — Make It Specific

The standfirst is a single sentence that states the article's argument — specific, declarative, and precise enough to be argued with.

This is where your article opening goes. It begins with a specific observation — a fact, a decision, a thing that was noticed. Not a question. Not a promise of what is to come. The reader encounters substance immediately.

Second paragraph advances the argument one step. It does not summarise the first paragraph. It builds on it. Each paragraph earns its position by moving the argument forward.

Your First Section Heading — Contains Target Keyword

The section opens on the claim it is going to make. Then it makes the case for that claim with specific evidence, named decisions, or documented reasoning. Abstract claims are not made. Every claim is specific.

"A pullquote pulls the single most precise, most arguable sentence from the section. It earns its position by being specific enough to be challenged."

Continue the argument. One idea per paragraph. The reader moves through at the speed the writing intends.

Your Second Section Heading

This section advances the argument from where the previous section left it. It does not repeat. It does not summarise. It goes one step further.

// Key point

A key point block isolates one specific, documented insight that the reader should carry with them after this article. It is precise. It is arguable. It is not a platitude.

The argument continues in the paragraphs below the key point block. Use as many paragraphs as the argument requires — not more.

The closing paragraph is a declarative statement of the logical implication of everything that preceded it. It does not summarise the article. It states the conclusion the article has earned. It ends when the thought is complete.
